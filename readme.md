# [Drone Formation Control] Behavior-Based Communication and Perception Formation Interference Detection and Avoidance in a Dynamic Multi-Agent System [with code included]

Introduction: Drones (UAVs) are increasingly being used in various applications, necessitating effective flocking coordination and navigation. In environments with interference zones, ensuring communication quality and avoiding obstacles becomes crucial [1]. This paper proposes a formation control strategy to address these challenges, enabling a group of drones to navigate towards their destination while avoiding interference zones. The strategy combines gradient controllers with mobile controllers, with the gradient controller responsible for achieving the desired formation and optimizing communication quality, and the mobile controller guiding the swarm to leave the interference zone and reach their destination [2] [3]. Its objective is to ensure efficient navigation, maintain communication within the swarm, and mitigate interference caused by interference zones. The main contribution of this paper lies in proposing a new formation control strategy for drone swarms, enabling them to successfully fly towards their designated destinations while avoiding unknown interference zones.
Li [2] first proposed the gradient controller responsible for achieving the desired formation. His method ensures that the swarm maintains a communication standard but does not consider any restrictions on its actual movement. In fact, drone deployment often occurs in locations where many hazardous obstacles hinder the success of the mission. Interference zones or jamming devices are commonly used to interfere with drones, as they rely on wireless communication [4]. There are numerous methods to avoid such interference, such as channel surfing, jamming-resistant nodes, and spatial avoidance [5]. Due to the increased maneuverability of drones, spatial avoidance is a common technique [6]. Therefore, researching robust drone interference avoidance strategies is significant. Xu proposed a method for navigating this environment with interference zones, but failed to maintain appropriate communication quality levels within the swarm [3]. By combining these two control strategies, a new control method was developed and experimentally validated. Simulation results demonstrate the effectiveness of this method.

Conclusions in summary, the proposed formation control strategy successfully enables a group of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) to navigate to a destination effectively while avoiding interference zones. By integrating gradient controllers for formation and motion controllers for avoiding interference areas, it demonstrates adaptive behavior that maintains communication quality and the required formation. Simulation experiment results validate the effectiveness of this method. The current approach has certain limitations, mainly regarding the fixed direction of agent maneuvers around interference positions. Additionally, by adding many purpose locations as checkpoints to guide the group through more complex environments, more complex path planning can be achieved. Efforts aimed at improving the impact of interference on communication quality have promising potential for further improvement. Further research and experiments can build upon these findings to enhance group coordination and navigation in complex environments.
